# Player Experience

## UX principle
The player has limited attention, perception, memory, and processing capacity. More text, arrows, markers, alerts, or effects do not automatically create more understanding.

## Immediate decision test
Every HUD/world cue must answer:

> What decision does the player need this information for right now?

If there is no clear answer, the element is a removal/simplification candidate.

## Desired behaviour
The road should teach the player to:
- scan traffic rather than stare at UI;
- predict openings;
- commit to a line;
- brake or abort when the opening closes;
- recognise main-road versus hẻm trade-offs from the world itself;
- understand why a crash happened.

## Current hypotheses
- The world is not yet doing enough guidance work; the HUD still carries too much explanatory burden.
- Traffic should create readable openings rather than arbitrary obstruction.
- Failure feedback must not obscure the road when the player most needs orientation.

## Onboarding rule
Teach through controllable situations before adding explanatory copy. Prefer world state, motion, spacing, sound, lighting, and simple feedback over stacking tutorial text.

## Validation
Observe whether a player can answer without explanation:
1. Where am I going?
2. What is dangerous right now?
3. What route options do I have?
4. Why did I crash?
5. What did I do well?

If the answer requires reading several HUD elements simultaneously, simplify or move the information into the world.
